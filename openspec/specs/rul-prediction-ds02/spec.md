# RUL Prediction (DS02) — Deployed Specification

**Capability:** `rul-prediction-ds02`
**Status:** Accepted — current truth as implemented in `02_DS02_ensemble_RUL.ipynb`
**Date:** 2026-10-19
**Source of record:** `02_DS02_ensemble_RUL.ipynb` (17 cells: 9 markdown, 8 code, all code
cells executed with no error outputs), generated by `scripts/build_02_notebook.py` (754 lines).

---

## Purpose

Predict remaining useful life (RUL) for N-CMAPSS **DS02** turbofan units from run-to-failure
telemetry, producing a single scalar RUL per engine window. The capability combines a
sequential champion (a bidirectional LSTM) with a tabular challenger (a quantile LightGBM)
over **physically residualized** features, reconciles them with an out-of-sample meta-learner
and a shrinkage factor, and smooths the result in a strictly causal way.

This document is the **deployed** contract. Where the current implementation is known not to
meet the deployability bar, the gap is recorded under
[Known limitations](#known-limitations) and is the subject of
[the 2026-10-19 hardening change](../../changes/2026-10-19-ds02-production-hardening/proposal.md).

### Requirement: Sensor residualization

The capability SHALL remove the variance each sensor owes to the flight regime before it
reaches the tabular model. For each of the 14 sensors it SHALL fit a `Ridge(alpha=1.0)` on a
degree-2 `PolynomialFeatures` expansion of the standardized operational channels
(`include_bias=False`, `4 -> 14` features), and SHALL store the residual
`Xs_raw - Ridge(Poly2(W_scaled))` after standardizing it with a dedicated `StandardScaler`.

The fitted objects SHALL be the polynomial transform, exactly 14 Ridge models and the
residual scaler, and they SHALL be bundled into a `SensorResidualizer` whose only operation is
`transform(W_scaled, Xs_raw)`.

#### Scenario: Residualization is applied per sensor

- GIVEN the standardized regime matrix and the raw sensor matrix
- WHEN `SensorResidualizer.transform` is called
- THEN 14 residuals are produced, one per sensor
- AND each is standardized using the scaler fit on the residual distribution

#### Scenario: Residuals replace raw sensors in the tabular branch

- GIVEN the tabular feature assembly
- WHEN descriptors are computed
- THEN every sensor-derived column is computed over **residualized**, not raw, sensors

### Requirement: Tabular feature contract

The capability SHALL build exactly **93** tabular features per window from the concatenation
`hstack([cyc_norm, op_feat, xs_feat])`:

| Group | Count | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cyc_norm` | 1 | `cycle / 100.0` |
| `op_feat` | 8 | `op.mean(axis=0)` concatenated with `op[-1]`, over the 4 `W` channels |
| `xs_feat` | 84 | 6 descriptors × 14 residualized sensors: `mean`, `last`, `std`, `xs[-10:].mean`, `(last - first)`, `(xs[-5:].mean - xs[:5:].mean)` |

The resulting matrices SHALL be `X_train_tab (17450, 93)` and `X_test_tab (6196, 93)`. The
representative counts in prose (`70 descriptors` + `14 slopes`) are the same 84 columns grouped

### Requirement: LightGBM quantile contract

The standalone tabular model SHALL be a `LGBMRegressor` with the objective set to
`quantile` and `alpha = 0.35`, using the exact configuration:

```python
LGBMRegressor(
    objective="quantile", alpha=0.35, n_estimators=300, learning_rate=0.02,
    num_leaves=15, max_depth=4, min_child_samples=50, reg_alpha=1.0, reg_lambda=5.0,
    colsample_bytree=0.8, subsample=0.8, random_state=42, verbose=-1,

```

The asymmetric quantile is deliberate: at `alpha = 0.35` the model is biased toward
*under*-predicting RUL, which is the physically conservative direction under the asymmetric
NASA score.

### Requirement: BiLSTM contract

The sequential champion SHALL consume `(50, 19)` windows: the 18 scaled `W + Xs` channels with
`cyc_norm` appended as a 19th channel on every timestep. The architecture SHALL be exactly:

1. `BatchNormalization` on the input window.
2. Two stacked `Bidirectional(LSTM(64, return_sequences=True))` layers, each followed by
   `Dropout(0.2)`.
3. Triple temporal pooling via `Concatenate([Lambda(lambda z: z[:, -1, :]), GlobalAveragePooling1D(), GlobalMaxPooling1D()])`,
   yielding `128 + 128 + 128 = 384` pooled dimensions.
4. `Dropout(0.2)` → `Dense(32, activation="relu")` → `Dense(1)`.

The model SHALL compile with `loss=Huber(delta=10.0)` and `Adam(learning_rate=1e-3)`, and SHALL
train with `batch_size=128`, `epochs=22`, `EarlyStopping(patience=5, restore_best_weights=True)`
and `ReduceLROnPlateau(factor=0.5, patience=2)`, under `SEED = 42`. The compiled model SHALL
report **154,253** total parameters (**154,215** trainable).

Windows SHALL be extracted with `WINDOW_SIZE = 50`, `step_train = 3` and `step_test = 2`,
producing train `(17450, 50, 19)` and test `(6196, 50, 19)`.

#### Scenario: Pooled head has 384 dimensions

- GIVEN the second bidirectional layer returns a sequence
- WHEN the three pooling outputs are concatenated
- THEN the concatenated width is 384
- AND the parameter count is 154,253 total / 154,215 trainable

#### Scenario: Cycle channel is present on every timestep

- GIVEN a window with a last-row cycle value
- WHEN the sequence is assembled
- THEN each of the 50 timesteps carries 19 values, the 19th being `cycle / 100.0`

#### Scenario: Training converges within the recorded budget

- GIVEN the training configuration above
- WHEN the recorded run executes
- THEN early stopping triggers at epoch 11 with a best epoch of 6
- AND total training time is 147.99 s

### Requirement: Out-of-sample stacking contract

The capability SHALL build meta-features `Z = [pred_lstm, pred_lgb]` and SHALL fit
two meta-learners on the validation holdout rows:

- a `RidgeCV(alphas=np.logspace(-2, 3, 20))`
- a `LGBMRegressor(max_depth=3, num_leaves=7, n_estimators=120, learning_rate=0.03, min_child_samples=20)`

The meta prediction SHALL be the equal blend `p_meta = 0.5 * ridge_meta.predict(Z) + 0.5 * lgb_meta.predict(Z)`,
which reduces the variance of the stacked estimate relative to either meta-learner alone.

The out-of-sample property SHALL hold for the **base models**: unit 16 SHALL never appear in
the fitting data of the BiLSTM or the standalone LightGBM, so its predictions are genuine
out-of-fold predictions. The property SHALL NOT be claimed for the meta-learner fit itself.

`cyc_norm` SHALL be offered to both base models — channel 19 of the BiLSTM sequence and
feature 93 of the LightGBM tabular summary — because a fleet does know how many cycles an
engine has flown. It SHALL NOT be offered to the stacking layer: when it was, the meta-learner
put ~100 % of its weight on it (ridge coef `[0.0019, 0.0124, -98.4065]`, LightGBM importances
`[0, 0, 720]`) and discarded both base models, so the "ensemble" was reading a counter. That
shortcut is also what made the hybrid's seed spread collapse to ±0.001 RMSE.

#### Scenario: Meta-features are the two predictions

- GIVEN validation predictions from both base models
- WHEN `Z` is assembled
- THEN it has exactly two columns, in the order `[pred_lstm, pred_lgb]`
- AND `cyc_norm` is not among them

#### Scenario: Meta prediction is an equal blend

- GIVEN a fitted `ridge_meta` and a fitted `lgb_meta`
- WHEN the meta prediction is produced
- THEN it is the arithmetic mean of the two meta-learner outputs

### Requirement: Beta shrinkage is selected out-of-sample

The capability SHALL reconcile the meta prediction with the BiLSTM through a shrinkage factor
`beta` in `[0, 1]`:

```text
p_raw = p_lstm + beta * (p_meta - p_lstm)
```

`beta` SHALL be selected by scanning a grid `beta = linspace(0, 1, 21)` and minimizing a
combined, scale-free criterion that weighs both error magnitudes:

```text
criterion(beta) = rmse_val(beta) / rmse_lstm_val + nasa_val(beta) / nasa_lstm_val
```

Each grid point SHALL be scored **after** applying the causal EMA, so the selected `beta`
optimizes the smoothed quantity that is actually reported. `beta = 0` SHALL be a legal
outcome and SHALL degrade the ensemble safely to the plain BiLSTM.

The selection SHALL be out-of-sample with respect to the base-model fitting data: the grid
SHALL be scored on unit 16, which neither base model saw. The recorded selection is
`opt_beta = 1.00`.

> ⚠️ **Residual risk:** the meta-learners are fit on the same unit-16 rows on which the grid is
> then scored, which biases unit-16 validation RMSE downward and pushes the selection toward
> the grid maximum. See [Known limitations](#known-limitations) and
> [the hardening change](../../changes/2026-10-19-ds02-production-hardening/spec.md).

#### Scenario: Grid is scored on the smoothed prediction

- GIVEN the validation predictions and a candidate `beta`
- WHEN the criterion for that `beta` is computed
- THEN the candidate blend is passed through `causal_ema_by_unit(..., span=5)` first
- AND the criterion divides both RMSE and NASA by their BiLSTM-only counterparts

#### Scenario: Standardized criterion is scale-free

- GIVEN validation RMSE and NASA at `beta = 0`
- WHEN the criterion is evaluated at `beta = 0`
- THEN it evaluates to `2.0`

#### Scenario: Recorded selection

- GIVEN the recorded run
- WHEN the grid is resolved
- THEN `opt_beta` is `1.00`
- AND the reported test row is labelled `Hybrid Stacking Ensemble (beta=1.00, EMA5)`

### Requirement: Causal EMA smoothing

The capability SHALL apply an exponential moving average **per unit**, over rows ordered by
cycle, so that no window can be influenced by a later cycle:

```python
idx = np.where(units == u)[0]
idx = idx[np.argsort(cycles[idx])]
out[idx] = pd.Series(pred[idx]).ewm(span=span, adjust=False).mean().values
```

The span SHALL be 5, `adjust=False` SHALL be used, and the smoothed predictions SHALL be
clipped at zero (`np.clip(p, 0, None)`), since RUL is non-negative. The same smoothing SHALL
be applied to the BiLSTM-only baseline whenever the two are compared, so the comparison is
like-for-like.

#### Scenario: Smoothing is strictly causal

- GIVEN predictions for one unit ordered by cycle
- WHEN the causal EMA is applied
- THEN the value at cycle *t* depends only on cycles `<= t`
- AND no value from a later cycle modifies it

#### Scenario: Units are smoothed independently

- GIVEN two units in the same prediction array
- WHEN smoothing runs
- THEN each unit's series is processed on its own index set
- AND no value crosses the unit boundary

#### Scenario: Baseline is smoothed with the same operator

- GIVEN the reported comparison table
- WHEN the BiLSTM-only row is produced
- THEN it is smoothed with `causal_ema_by_unit(..., span=5)`
- AND is labelled `BiLSTM 2-Layer + EMA causal (span=5)`

### Requirement: Metric contract

The capability SHALL report four metrics computed on the test units `[11, 14, 15]`:
RMSE `= sqrt(mean_squared_error(y_true, y_pred))`, MAE `= mean_absolute_error(y_true, y_pred)`,
R² `= r2_score(y_true, y_pred)`, and the NASA score:

```python
def calculate_nasa_score(y_true, y_pred):
    d = np.asarray(y_pred).ravel() - np.asarray(y_true).ravel()
    score = np.where(d < 0, np.exp(-d / 13.0) - 1.0, np.exp(d / 10.0) - 1.0)
    return float(np.sum(score))
```

Two properties SHALL be preserved and stated wherever the metric is used:

1. **The NASA score is a SUM over rows, not a mean.** Its value therefore scales with the
   number of scored windows (6196 on this test split) and is comparable only across models
   evaluated on the same rows.
2. **No 125-cycle clamp is applied on the DS02 path.** The clamp exists only in the FD001
   fallback loader and MUST NOT be introduced into the DS02 pipeline.

#### Scenario: NASA score is a sum

- GIVEN a set of scored windows
- WHEN the NASA score is computed
- THEN the per-row asymmetric penalty is summed, not averaged
- AND the reported value is a single float

#### Scenario: Asymmetry direction is correct

- GIVEN one window where the prediction underestimates RUL (`d < 0`)
- WHEN its penalty is computed
- THEN the penalty uses the gentler `exp(-d / 13.0) - 1.0` branch
- AND a same-magnitude overestimate uses the steeper `exp(d / 10.0) - 1.0` branch

#### Scenario: No clamp on DS02 targets

- GIVEN DS02 test targets above 125 cycles
- WHEN metrics are computed
- THEN the targets are used unmodified

### Requirement: Calibrated prediction interval

The calibration rows SHALL be out-of-fold with respect to the meta-learner: every row scored

estimate unchanged.

upper bound. A band that reaches its nominal coverage by being arbitrarily wide SHALL be
described as a bound rather than an interval.

## Known limitations

These are recorded gaps between the current implementation and the deployability bar. Each is
in scope for [the 2026-10-19 hardening change](../../changes/2026-10-19-ds02-production-hardening/proposal.md).

| # | Limitation | Evidence | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `HybridStackingEnsemble` and `SensorResidualizer` are defined inside the notebook, so the pickle references `__main__` | Both classes are notebook cells; `joblib.dump(ensemble, 'ensemble.joblib')` serializes them | Module 1 of the artifact is not loadable in a fresh process without importing the classes first |
| 2 | The Keras `Lambda` layer wraps a bare Python lambda, and binds the artifact to one Python minor version | `layers.Lambda(lambda z: z[:, -1, :], name="last_step")`; Keras serializes it as marshalled bytecode, and `marshal`'s code-object format is version-specific | Reproduced: the artifact loads under Python 3.10 but raises `ValueError: bad marshal data (unknown type code)` under the repository's Python 3.12, even with the `__main__` dependency shimmed away |
| 3 | Beta calibration is in-sample with respect to the meta-learner | `ridge_meta`/`lgb_meta` are fit on unit-16 rows and the grid is scored on those same rows (validation RMSE 0.308 vs BiLSTM 4.221) | Forces `opt_beta -> 1.00`; the "out-of-sample" claim holds for the base models only |
| 4 | Preprocessing objects are fit on all six train units, unit 16 included | `scaler`, `scaler_w`, `poly_w`, the 14 `sensor_ridges` and `res_scaler` are all fit on the full train arrays before the unit-16 split | Unit 16 is excluded from *model* fitting but not from *preprocessing* fitting; the "no leakage" property is guaranteed only against test units 11/14/15 |
| 5 | BiLSTM results vary materially across seeds | Seed 42: RMSE 5.447; seed 7: RMSE 6.564; dual-pooling baseline 5.789 | A single-seed result MUST NOT be presented as a stable estimate |
| 6 | Compute starvation invalidated some probe comparisons | One probe epoch took 1553 s | Comparisons across runs with different wall-clock budgets are not reliable |
| 7 | The recorded outputs were produced under the system Python 3.10, not under the repository `.venv` | Saved tracebacks point at `…\Python310\lib\site-packages\…`; `.venv` is Python 3.12.10 with LightGBM 4.7.0 and scikit-learn 1.9.1, against 4.6.0 and 1.5.1 in the execution environment. Root cause: no kernelspec is registered for the `.venv`, so the only resolvable `python3` kernel is the system 3.10 one; the FD001 notebook's `display_name` reads `.venv (3.12.10)` while its `name` still resolves to 3.10 | The recorded metrics are not reproducible from the repository environment as-is, and the notebooks cannot be run in the environment the project ships |
| 8 | The repository is not under version control, and a previously extracted module was lost | `__pycache__/ds02_ensemble.cpython-312.pyc` (compiled 2026-10-19 22:07) has no surviving `.py` source; there is no `.git` directory | No rollback and no history; the baseline artefacts have to be copied by hand (task 0.1) |
| 9 | `predict(..., units=None)` silently disables per-unit smoothing | `HybridStackingEnsemble.predict` substitutes a single pseudo-unit when `units` is omitted, collapsing the whole batch into one EMA series | Plausible-looking but wrong predictions for any caller that omits `units` |
| 10 | `WINDOW_SIZE = 50` overstates the temporal horizon | `STRIDE = 100` leaves roughly 114 samples per flight cycle, so 50 samples span about 0.44 flight cycles | Reasoning about the model's memory from the name alone is wrong by two orders of magnitude |
| 11 | The FD001 fallback is silent and writes to the same artifact path | `load_rul_dataset()` falls back to the C-MAPSS text files when the DS02 HDF5 is absent, and the notebook then saves an FD001 model to `ensemble.joblib` with the identical `paradigm` metadata string | A 16-feature FD001 model can silently replace an 18-feature DS02 one; only `n_features_in_` and `n_op` distinguish them, and neither appears in the file name or the console output |

The full technical audit, with the verification evidence for every item (including the
reproduced `AttributeError` when loading `ensemble.joblib` in a clean process), is recorded in
`docs/02_DS02_ensemble_RUL_spec.md` section 6.

### Measured metrics after the change (test units 11/14/15, 6 196 windows)

Mean ± standard deviation over seeds `[42, 7, 123]`:

| Model | RMSE | MAE | R² | NASA score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LightGBM standalone | 7.098 ± 0.034 | 5.572 ± 0.034 | 0.8557 ± 0.0014 | 5772.5 ± 50.1 |

| Hybrid stacking + EMA5 | 5.523 ± 0.001 | 4.795 ± 0.001 | 0.9126 ± 0.0001 | 3435.6 ± 0.7 |

### Newly recorded limitation: the cycle counter dominates the meta-learner

With `beta = 1.0` the final prediction is the meta-learner output, and the shipped
meta-learner places ~100 % of its weight on `cyc_norm`
(`ridge coef = [0.0019, 0.0124, -98.4065]`, `lgb_meta importances = [0, 0, 720]`).
Measured consequences (`models/ablation_meta_cycle.json`):

| Configuration | RMSE | MAE | NASA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cycle available (shipped) | 5.522 | 4.795 | 3435.3 |
| cycle removed from every component | 5.568 | 3.742 | 3233.3 |
| OLS on cycle alone | 14.327 | 13.148 | 20267.6 |

The clock is not the source of the accuracy — a cycle-only regression scores 14.33 —
but when it is offered the learner takes it and discards both base models, which is
why the hybrid's seed-to-seed standard deviation is 0.001. **The cycle-free
configuration is the recommended deployment target**; it is kept as a measured
variant rather than silently adopted, because the published metrics describe the
shipped one.
